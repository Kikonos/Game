FTL.1) for CAT Aeroplane Pilots and Cabin Crew —
Reference Document (current as of 22 October
2026)
For EU scheduled and charter commercial air transport (CAT) aeroplane operations, the
binding rules are still the 2014 Subpart FTL (Regulation (EU) No 965/2012 Annex III, as
inserted by Regulation (EU) No 83/2014, applicable since 18 February 2016)             skybrary   plus
CS FTL.1.   europa     luftfartstilsynet   Pilots and cabin crew share the same FDP tables,
cumulative limits and rest rules. Three differences matter: cabin crew FDP may be extended
by up to 1 hour of earlier reporting (ORO.FTL.205(c));         raven   cabin crew have their own in-
flight rest table (CS FTL.1.205(c)(3)); and only flight crew augmentation unlocks long-haul
extensions.   luftfartstilsynet



TL;DR
   Core limits (unchanged since 2016): basic max FDP for acclimatised crew is 13:00 at
   best (1–2 sectors, reporting 06:00–13:29), falling to 9:00. Planned extension +1 h, max
   twice per 7 days; augmented crew 14–17 h (+1 h for a single sector over 9 h);
   commander's discretion +2 h (+3 h augmented). Duty 60/110/190 h per 7/14/28 days;
   flight time 100 h/28 days, 900 h/calendar year, 1,000 h/12 months. Rest ≥ preceding
   duty, minimum 12 h at home base or 10 h away (incl. 8 h sleep opportunity); 36 h reset
   incl. 2 local nights at most every 168 h.
   Cabin crew follow pilot rules except: FDP can be longer by the reporting-time
   difference (max 1 h) while still based on the flight crew's report time; minimum in-flight
   rest comes from a separate CS FTL.1.205(c)(3) table up to an 18:00 FDP (class 3 not
   allowed above 16:00, class 2 not above 17:00).
   2026 status: no change to scheduled/charter numbers; the only CS change since 2016
   is ED Decision 2023/023/R (appropriate FRM for late finishes and long night duties,
   three night-duty subtypes). New FTL for air taxi, AEMS and single-pilot operations
   (EASA Opinion No 02/2026) was adopted by the Commission as a delegated act on 8
   September 2026. Council document 12992/26 (Brussels, 10 September 2026) states
   that "It shall apply from 15 October 2028"; EASA's Opinion had proposed 1 September
   2028. It is not yet in force. UK mirrors the 2016 EU rules without the 2023 amendment;
   UAE (GCAA) and FAA Part 117 use materially different numbers.

Operator-specific (approved IFTSS) flag. Every CS FTL.1 number, and everything stated
as "the scheme shall specify", can differ for your operator. The competent authority
approves each operator's Individual Flight Time Specification Scheme (IFTSS), which may
deviate from CS FTL.1 (Article 76(7) of Regulation (EU) 2018/1139, formerly Article 22(2) of
Regulation 216/2008) or derogate from the implementing rule (Article 71 of 2018/1139).
Collective labour agreements may be more protective. The operator's Operations Manual
Part A, chapter 7, always prevails for the individual crew member.

Scope and exclusions

   Subpart FTL applies to flight crew and cabin crew (not other service personnel).
     europa    CS FTL.1 applies to scheduled and charter operations, excluding EMS, air taxi
   and single-pilot operations (CS FTL.1.100).         luftfartstilsynet

   Under Article 8 of Regulation 965/2012, air taxi (non-scheduled on-demand CAT with
   aeroplanes of MOPSC 19 or less; Article 2(6)), aeroplane EMS and single-pilot CAT
   aeroplane operations remain under Subpart Q of Annex III to Regulation (EEC) No
   3922/91 ("EU-OPS Subpart Q") plus national law. Helicopter CAT (including HEMS) is
   under national law.     europa +3

   Subpart Q (13 h basic FDP, −30 min per sector from the third, max −2 h, minus WOCL
   encroachment) is now legacy.        wordpress   EASA Opinion No 02/2026, §2.1, states:
   "Subpart Q has some fundamental gaps: it does not establish a maximum daily FDP for
   single-pilot and AEMS operations, and it does not provide for standby, in-flight rest and
   split duty", leaving "no uniform level of safety, and no level playing field".

Effectiveness reviews (Article 9b)

   Phase 1 (published 28 February 2019): increased probability of high fatigue on night
   duties and late finishes, for pilots and cabin crew;     europa   led to ED Decision
   2023/023/R.    europa

   Phase 2, "FTL 2.0" (EASA.2019.C31; Deliverable 2.6, 23 April 2025). NLR ran it with DLR,
   FIOH, Stockholm University and Jeppesen; per NLR's 9 July 2025 release it followed
   more than 220 flight and cabin crew at eight airlines. Findings:
       "the basic set-up in the regulation of limiting the maximum basic FDP duration for
        acclimatised crew as a function of the start time of the duty and workload (the
        number of sectors) is sound";
        fatigue rises with FDP length and sectors (higher for more than 6 sectors);
        highest fatigue on overnight FDPs over 11 h in an unknown state of acclimatisation
        (point estimate close to 0.7, few observations);
        "38% of the pilots reported using controlled rest".

     No rule change from FTL 2.0 had been adopted as of October 2026. NLR's release says
     the study recommended "expanding current requirements for appropriate FRM and
     applying it to all flight duty periods lasting ten hours or more" and keeping the 18-hour
     awake cap for standby.

3. Responsibilities, FRM, IFTSS and derogations
Operator responsibilities (ORO.FTL.110):

   publish rosters sufficiently in advance (AMC: 14 days);
    plan FDPs so crew remain sufficiently free from fatigue;
    set reporting times allowing for ground duties;
    consider the cumulative effects of long duty with minimum rest;
    avoid seriously disruptive patterns such as alternating day/night duties;
    apply the ARO.OPS.230 disruptive type;
    provide sufficient rest;
    plan and notify recurrent extended recovery rest;
    plan FDPs to be achievable;
    (j) change a schedule or crew arrangements if actual operations exceed the
    maximum FDP on more than 33 % of the flight duties in that schedule during a
    scheduled seasonal period, monitored through roster-robustness indicators
    (AMC1/GM1 ORO.FTL.110(j)).

Crew member responsibilities (ORO.FTL.115): comply with CAT.GEN.MPA.100(b) (do not
operate when unfit or fatigued); make optimum use of rest opportunities and facilities.
 luftfartstilsynet


Fatigue risk management (ORO.FTL.120):

    When required, FRM is part of the management system and described in the Operations
    Manual. It covers policy, documentation, scientific principles, hazard identification
    (predictive, proactive, reactive), risk mitigation, safety assurance and promotion
    (guidance: ICAO Doc 9966).
    A fully-fledged FRM is mandatory for Table 4 (ORO.FTL.205(b)(3)), for reduced rest (CS
    FTL.1.235(c)(2))    europa   and in practice for any CS deviation.
    "Appropriate FRM" is required for night duties over 10 h and late finishes (CS
    FTL.1.205(a)(2)).   europa


Flight time specification schemes / IFTSS (ORO.FTL.125):

    Schemes must comply with the Basic Regulation, Subpart FTL and other law including
    Directive 2000/79/EC, and be approved before use.
    Deviations from the CS require a full description and safety assessment (former Article
    22(2) of Regulation 216/2008, now Article 76(7) of 2018/1139).
    Derogations from the implementing rule follow former Article 14(6) (now Article 71).
    Within 2 years of a deviation or derogation, the operator must analyse data scientifically
    and report to the authority (ORO.FTL.125(d); ARO.OPS.235(d)).        luftfartstilsynet


Collective agreements. Recital (4) of Regulation 83/2014 and the EASA FAQ confirm that
more protective national social legislation and collective labour agreements are not
precluded.     europa




4. Home base (ORO.FTL.200 / CS FTL.1.200) and
acclimatisation
   The operator assigns a home base to each crew member (ORO.FTL.200): a single
   airport location assigned with a high degree of permanence (CS FTL.1.200(a)).

   new base increases to 72 h, including 3 local nights; travel between bases is
   positioning (CS FTL.1.200(b); ORO.FTL.235(e)(3)).                    luftfartstilsynet

   GM1 CS FTL.1.200: consider temporary accommodation nearer base if the commute
   usually exceeds 90 minutes.           luftfartstilsynet

11.4 Reserve (ORO.FTL.230, CS FTL.1.230)
   Reserve must be rostered. The scheme sets the maximum reserve period duration and

12. Rest periods (ORO.FTL.235, CS FTL.1.235)

12.1 Minimum rest
   Home base (ORO.FTL.235(a)): ≥ preceding duty or 12 h, whichever greater. If the
   operator provides suitable accommodation at home base, the away minimum may apply
   ("back-to-back", GM1 ORO.FTL.235(a)(2)).
   Away (ORO.FTL.235(b)): ≥ preceding duty or 10 h, incl. an 8 h sleep opportunity plus
   travel and physiological needs. AMC1: physiological needs = 1 h; if travel to
   accommodation exceeds 30 min, rest increases by twice the excess.         luftfartstilsynet

   Special cases:
        after a planned extension: +2 h pre/post or +4 h post (ORO.FTL.205(d));
        after an augmented FDP: ≥ duty or 14 h (CS FTL.1.205(c)(6));
        after commander's discretion: never below 10 h (ORO.FTL.205(f)(1)(iii)).


12.2 Reduced rest (ORO.FTL.235(c), CS FTL.1.235(c))
    Only under FRM.
    Minimum 12 h at home base, 10 h away.
    The next rest is extended, and the next FDP reduced, by the difference between the
    normal minimum and the reduced rest.
    Max 2 reduced rests between recurrent extended recovery rests; split duty may not
    follow.
    In practice it only bites after duties longer than 12 h at base or 10 h away.


12.3 Recurrent extended recovery rest (ORO.FTL.235(d))
    Minimum 36 h including 2 local nights.
    No more than 168 h between the end of one and the start of the next.
    Increased to 2 local days twice every month.
    Applies to reserve crew too (GM1 CS FTL.1.230(c)).

13. Nutrition, records and training
   Nutrition (ORO.FTL.240): a meal and drink opportunity during the FDP, especially
   when it exceeds 6 h. The Operations Manual sets minimum duration and meal windows
   (AMC1 example: an FDP from 11:00 to 22:00 gives two meal opportunities).
   Records (ORO.FTL.245), kept 24 months:
         flight times;
         start, duration and end of each duty period and FDP;
         rest periods and days free of duty;
         assigned home base;
         reports on extended FDPs and reduced rest.
         Copies go on request to the crew member or another operator. Records for crew
         working for more than one operator are also kept 24 months.

   Fatigue management training (ORO.FTL.250, AMC1): initial and recurrent, for crew,
   rostering staff and managers. Syllabus:
         regulatory requirements;
         sleep and circadian basics;
         causes and effects of fatigue;
       countermeasures;
       lifestyle;
       sleep disorders;
       long-range and heavy short-haul effects;
       multiple time zones;
       crew responsibility for rest;
       since 2023, optimum use of sleep opportunities before and during disruptive duties.




14. Worked examples (standard CS FTL.1; check your IFTSS)
Example 1 — Acclimatised, 2 sectors, report 06:00.

   Table 2, row 06:00–13:29, 1–2 sectors → max FDP 13:00, ending by 19:00 on-blocks.
   Planned extension: CS FTL.1.205(b) row 06:00–06:14 → Not allowed. A 07:00 report
   would allow 14:00, subject to the 2-in-7 limit and extra rest.
   Commander's discretion → up to 15:00.

Example 2 — Long-haul, 3 pilots, class 1 bunk, 1 sector of 12 h.

   CS FTL.1.205(c)(2)(i)(C) → 16:00, plus (c)(4) → 17:00.
   Each pilot needs ≥ 90 consecutive min rest; the landing pilots ≥ 2 consecutive h.
   Discretion up to +3 h. Destination rest ≥ max(preceding duty, 14 h).
   With 4 pilots and class 1: 17:00, or 18:00 with the long-sector bonus.

Example 3 — Cabin crew on Example 2.

   Flight crew max 17:00 → cabin crew in class 1, band 16:31–17:00 → 3:00 minimum each.
   Class 2 seats → 3:25; class 3 → not allowed above 16:00.
   If cabin crew report 1 h earlier, their FDP may be 18:00 from their own report; band
   17:31–18:00 (class 1 only) → 3:50 each.

Example 4 — Cabin crew reporting 1 h earlier (short-haul).

   Flight crew report 07:00, 3 sectors → 12:30, ending 19:30.
   Cabin crew report 06:00 → max FDP still based on 07:00 (12:30) plus the 1:00
   difference = 13:30 from 06:00, ending 19:30.
   Reporting at 05:30 (1:30 earlier) still allows only +1:00: 13:30, ending 19:00.

Example 5 — Split duty.
   Report 08:00, 4 sectors → 12:00.
   Ground time 4:30 with 30 min allotted to duties and travel → net break 4:00 → +2:00 →
   max FDP 14:00.
   Accommodation is required; suitable accommodation only if the break is ≥ 6 h or
   touches the WOCL.
   Not allowed after reduced rest or with a planned extension.

Example 6 — Home standby.

   Other standby 07:00–23:00 (16 h). Called 14:00, reports 15:00 for 2 sectors.
   Standby 8 h → FDP reduced by 2 h. Table 2 row 15:00–15:29 → 12:00 − 2:00 = 10:00,
   ending 01:00.
   Awake check: woken 06:30 → 01:00 = 18:30, beyond the 18 h awake procedure, so the
   operator must shorten the FDP or provide rest.
   Duty counted for ORO.FTL.210: 25 % × 8 h = 2 h plus the FDP.

Example 7 — Rest after a rotation.

   Rotation resting 9 h behind home base, returning 80 h after first report → CS
   FTL.1.235(b)(3)(i) row ≤ 9, column 72:00–95:59 → 3 local nights at home base.
   Away from base after a 9:00 duty with a 50 min hotel transfer: 10:00 + 2 × 20 min =
   10:40 (AMC1 ORO.FTL.235(b)).
