# R3 — Real-world flavour & case studies for an ACMI / charter airline sim

Angle 3 of the ACMI research pack. Scope: operator profiles, famous operations, daily problems, the cabin-crew view, naming and jargon, then **37 contract archetypes** and **44 event ideas**.
Conventions: **(est.)** marks a designer estimate or a figure I could not confirm in a primary source. **(unverified)** marks something widely repeated that I did not confirm. Real companies are described as history only. Section 5 gives fictional stand-ins for use in the game.

---

## 0. Ten design takeaways

1. **Money does not come from the aircraft. It comes from saying yes fast.** Titan Airways sells "launch an aircraft within 60 minutes" sub-charter standby ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Airways)). Urgency should multiply price, and having a standby aircraft plus a rested crew is what earns that multiplier.
2. **Permits, crew hours and fuel are the three hidden clocks.** Each contract has a permit lead time (72 h to 14 working days), an FDP limit (13 h basic under EASA), and fuel or tech-stop constraints such as Antarctica (no fuel at destination) or Dili (777 not allowed to land, so a 767 with a tech stop went instead).
3. **Seasonality is the heartbeat.** European summer is peak (GetJet: July about 5,000 operated hours per month). Winter means parking aircraft, doing heavy checks, or chasing southern-hemisphere and Canadian winter leases. **Hajj moves about 11 days earlier every year** (late May in 2026, mid-May in 2027, and into April by 2029–2030). It is now colliding with the European summer ramp-up, so widebody rates should spike in those years.
4. **Reputation has two axes: "reliable" and "clean".** Deportation, prisoner and some military work pays steadily but costs crew morale and public reputation. GlobalX had ICE as more than half of its revenue, plus a diplomatic row with Brazil. Privilege Style quit UK Home Office flights after pressure.
5. **Governments are great clients until the invoice.** They pay well, pay late, change things at the last minute and sue. Model a client "payment reliability" stat.
6. **Your crews live abroad.** A wet-lease season puts whole crews in a client's city for months (GetJet: 6 aircraft at Hamburg for Eurowings). Hotel quality, per diems and time home drive morale.
7. **The aircraft is the brand you do not own.** Under ACMI you fly in someone else's colours or with their stickers, under their flight numbers, to their service standard. Passengers complain to the client and the client complains to you.
8. **Old widebodies are opportunity machines.** EuroAtlantic flew the last L-1011 in European passenger service. Hi Fly made an ex-Singapore A380 famous. Air Atlanta still makes money from 747s. Cheap old aircraft give high margins on ad hoc work but bring AOG risk.
9. **Every crisis is a contract.** Kabul, COVID, Sudan, Ukraine, earthquakes. Lead time is hours to days, prices are high and the risk is real (war-risk insurance, closed airports, staging hubs).
10. **Sub-chartering is a core loop.** When you cannot fly a job (AOG, crew, permit), you buy capacity from a rival and keep the client happy at a margin hit. That is what "sub-service" means and what brokers live on.

## 1. Operator profiles (the real cast, for inspiration)

### 1.1 EuroAtlantic Airways (Portugal), the user's former airline
- **Codes:** IATA **YU**, ICAO **MMZ**, callsign **"EUROATLANTIC"**. HQ in Carnaxide, base at Lisbon (LIS). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroAtlantic_Airways)
- **History:** Founded on 25 Aug 1993 as **Air Zarco** (Madeira roots: Zarco was the navigator who found Madeira), then traded as **Air Madeira**, and renamed **euroAtlantic** on 17 May 2000. Founder Tomaz Metello owned it until **2019**, when it was sold to the Luxembourg group **Imperial Jet / I‑Jet** (Abed El‑Jaouni). It owns **38% of STP Airways** (São Tomé and Príncipe).
- **Fleet over time:** L‑1011‑500 TriStar 2000–2010, described as the **last TriStar in European passenger service** (retired March 2010). Three 737‑300s 2000–06. Two 757‑200s 2007–11. 767‑300ER from 2008 (also a 767 BDSF freighter). A 767‑200ER wet-leased in from Jet Asia in 2014. A Citation CJ3 for exclusive flights 2015–18. **777‑200ER from 2019**. A single 737‑800 in 2020 only. **First Airbus (A330‑200 CS‑TGD) in 2025**, then an A330‑300 (CS‑TGI). In Sept 2025: 767‑300ER **CS‑TST, CS‑TSU, CS‑TSV**; 777‑200ER **CS‑TSX, CS‑TSW**. ch-aviation (2022) counted 4×767 plus 3 inactive 777s ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/118542-portugals-euroatlantic-airways-retires-only-b737-800)). The user's era of "2×767 + 2×777" fits roughly 2019–2023.
- **Notable operations:**
  - **Jan 2008:** Lisbon–Dili (East Timor) on a 757 with **140 GNR** (Portuguese gendarmerie) troops. It was the first aircraft larger than a 737 to land at Dili.
  - **2009:** Two 777s flown for **Biman Bangladesh**. **2014:** Two 767s for **Air Canada**.
  - **COVID 2020:** Repatriated **1,110 Portuguese** from CPLP (Portuguese-speaking) countries, often carrying humanitarian cargo on the outbound leg ([Lusa via Ver Angola](https://www.verangola.net/va/pt/042020/Sociedade/19568/Milhares-de-portugueses-regressam-a-casa-em-voos-que-transportam-ajuda.htm)). The **Dili repatriation** (200+ people) used a **767**: it lacked range for a nonstop flight, but the 777 that had the range "does not land in Dili" ([DN](https://www.dn.pt/pais/quando-em-timor-viram-um-aviao-portugues-foi-um-alivio-um-aconchego-12039115.html); [company PDF](https://www.euroatlantic.pt/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/pt_03_04_2020.pdf)). This is a perfect game example of the aircraft-to-airport fit problem. It also flew **US citizens from Luanda to Houston**, chained after a **Thailand–UK** repatriation ([Ver Angola](https://www.verangola.net/va/en/032020/Transports/19091/Covid-19-Portuguese-EuroAtlantic-repatriation-plane-from-Angola-to-USA.htm)).
  - **Regular customers:** TAP, **LOT** (777 on Warsaw–JFK LO26/27 until Sept 2025, and Warsaw–Toronto 6×/week from June 2026), **flynas (Hajj)**, TACV Cabo Verde, TUI fly Belgium, **Azul** (Orlando–Belo Horizonte with CS‑TSU, Nov 2025). Also makeshift cargo 767/777 flights for MENA Cargo (Bahrain) ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/109215-portugals-euroatlantic-partners-bahrains-mena-cargo)).
  - Sports-team and state-visit charters are typical for the type of operator but **unverified** for euroAtlantic specifically.
- **Game story:** "The small Lisbon widebody operator of the Lusophone world." Its natural network is Lisbon to Luanda, Maputo, Bissau, Praia/Sal, São Tomé, Dili and Brazil (Fortaleza, Recife, Salvador, Belo Horizonte). It survives by flying anything: flag-carrier gaps, Hajj, repatriation, cargo-in-cabin.

### 1.2 GetJet Airlines (Lithuania) and GetJet Malta / Airhub Airlines (Malta)
- **GetJet:** Founded **2016 in Vilnius** by **Aleksandr Celiadin**. First flight May 2016 with **one 737‑400** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GetJet_Airlines)). Now about **15–17 aircraft** (737‑800, A320, A321), an **800-person organisation** and crews of **15 nationalities**. Cabin crew are trained from zero at the group's own EASA training organisation (Airhub Training). The company targets 10–15% annual growth, to a fleet of about 20 ([aeroTELEGRAPH interview](https://www.aerotelegraph.com/with-such-a-capable-team-we-could-integrate-a-space-shuttle-into-our-aoc-and-no-one-would-be-surprised)). Reported 2025 revenue: **€165 m** ([TravelDailyNews](https://www.traveldailynews.com/aviation/getjet-airlines-reports-165m-euros-revenue-in-2025/)).
  - **Clients:** About **30 airline clients a year**, e.g. Wizz Air, Transavia, TUI, TAP, Air Arabia, Air Serbia, AZAL, airBaltic, Vueling, Icelandair, Marabu, Air Malta.
  - **How aircraft and crews deploy abroad:**
    - **Eurowings**, long-term: **6 aircraft based at Hamburg** from March 2026 for the summer peak ([aviation24](https://www.aviation24.be/acmi-operators/getjet-airlines/getjet-airlines-secures-long-term-acmi-deal-with-eurowings/amp/)).
    - **Canada** winter work: **Sunwing**, one aircraft plus crew, Nov 2019–Feb 2020 to Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Jamaica and Mexico. This needed Canadian Transportation Agency approval, a **20% cap** on wet-leased capacity, and a reciprocity check ([CTA A‑2019‑198](https://otc-cta.gc.ca/eng/ruling/a-2019-198)).
    - **Swoop** 737‑800 out of Toronto from 2023.
    - Foreign crew are exempt from Canadian work permits **only on direct international flights, not if there are domestic stops** ([immigration.ca](https://immigration.ca/guidance-for-officers-on-assessing-reciprocal-employment-and-entries-via-wet-lease/)). That makes a good visa and route-planning rule.
  - **Business shape:** About **half the fleet is on year-round long-term contracts**. The rest flies short-term summer deals, and in winter the company "minimises fixed costs" and rests crews. **July is the peak at about 5,000 operated hours a month.** Widebody A330/A340 flying (Hajj/Umrah, COVID vaccine cargo) **was stopped after the pandemic**.
  - **Pain points, in its own words:** OEM engine reliability problems, crew shortages after COVID, and rivals offering "unsustainable prices". Passengers complain that wet-lease crews **don't speak the local language**, aircraft don't match the client's standard, and there is little local knowledge. Unions criticise low pay at Eastern European ACMI operators. The company tries to **minimise registration changes and crew rotations**, which are a real cost.
- **Airhub Airlines (Malta, 9H‑), now becoming "GetJet Airlines Malta":** 2022 fleet ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/115358-maltas-airhub-airlines-seeks-more-used-narrowbodies); [aviation24](https://www.aviation24.be/acmi-operators/airhub-airlines/airhub-aviation-adds-second-airbus-a340-300-eyes-fleet-expansion/)):
  - 2 A330‑300. One was on a long-term ACMI with **Corendon**. The other, **9H‑AHQ**, was set up as a **"zero-LOPA" freighter**, meaning a passenger aircraft with no seats.
  - 3 A340‑300. One was wet-leased to **Air Sénégal**, one to **Surinam Airways**, and one was stored. Several were stripped to carry cargo during COVID.
  - 4 A320s, previously sub-leased to GetJet and **Norwegian Air Sweden**.
  - An **A330‑900 (9H‑SZN)** came in June 2022 when Airhub took over **Air Sénégal's ACMI contract from Hi Fly Malta**. That is a real example of a contract changing hands between ACMI providers.
- **Game story:** "The Baltic narrowbody machine." It has many small contracts, a crew of many nationalities, aircraft parked at client bases for whole seasons, a Maltese second AOC used for flexibility, and an in-house training pipeline.

### 1.3 Hi Fly (Portugal + Hi Fly Malta)
- Founded **2005** by **Paulo Mirpuri** (Mirpuri group); ICAO HFY. It is an all-Airbus widebody ACMI specialist (A330, A340, A380, plus A320 family). It had about 20 aircraft in 2019 and was the **first A330neo ACMI operator (2019)** ([Airbus via ADS](https://www.adsgroup.org.uk/knowledge/industry-member-news/airbus-delivers-first-a330neo-in-hi-fly-livery/)). It has since **exited the A340** after about 20 years ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/165939-hi-fly-exits-a340-operations-after-nearly-20-years)).
- **A380 9H‑MIP** ("MIP" for Mirpuri): MSN 006, ex‑Singapore Airlines 9V‑SKC. Hi Fly took it on in **2018** in a **"Save the Coral Reefs"** livery. Main customer: **Norwegian, Gatwick–JFK**. It also flew for Condor and others and **helped with the Thomas Cook collapse repatriation (2019)**. In Jan 2020 it flew the **Wuhan evacuation, chartered by France and other EU states** ([TPG](https://thepointsguy.com/news/hifly-a380-wuhan-evacuation/)). In COVID its seats were stripped to make it a freighter, but it **flew only 2 cargo flights**. It was retired in **Dec 2020**, the **first A380 retired twice** ([Flightradar24 blog](https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/flight-tracking-news/special-event/hi-fly-says-bye-bye-to-its-a380-with-love/); [ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/98480-hi-fly-malta-ends-a380-operations)).
- **Antarctica:** The **first A340 ever to land in Antarctica** came on **2 Nov 2021**: **9H‑SOL** from **Cape Town to Wolf's Fang**, a blue-ice runway **3,000 m × 60 m**. Flight time was **5–5.5 h** and the aircraft spent **under 3 h on the ground**. Captain **Carlos Mirpuri** flew it. Hi Fly repeated the trip in 2022 and 2023 with **9H‑TQZ** ([Sam Chui](https://samchui.com/2021/11/22/hi-fly-lands-first-airbus-a340-in-antarctica/); [Aviacionline](https://www.aviacionline.com/hi-fly-flew-again-to-antarctica-with-an-airbus-a340-300)).
- **COVID record:** An A340 flew **Montevideo–Melbourne nonstop** (Apr 2020), chartered for stranded cruise-ship passengers (Aurora Expeditions) ([TPG](https://thepointsguy.com/news/hi-fly-repatriation-longest-flight-a340)).
- **Refugees:** An **A330 flew Islamabad–Halifax (Jan 2024) with 313 Afghan refugees**, chartered via **IOM** and a charter broker ([CTA record](https://portail-portal.otc-cta.gc.ca/en/fifth-seventh-freedom-charter-flights-approved-by-agency/434827)).
- **Recent:** An A330‑300 for **South African Airways on São Paulo–Johannesburg**, 17 Nov 2025 to 11 Feb 2026 (Passageiro de Primeira).
- **Game story:** "The showman." It flies headline aircraft, does record flights and makes green-livery PR. A single halo aircraft (A380) brings prestige and press, but a pandemic can kill it.

### 1.4 Wamos Air (Spain)
- Madrid based, formerly **Pullmantur Air**, the cruise group's airline. It flew **747‑400s until 2020**. In **2022** it dropped all scheduled flying to do **only charter and ACMI**, passenger and cargo. Its fleet is all widebody: about **4 A330‑200 + 5 A330‑300** ([Wamos](https://wamosair.com/about-us/); [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Wamos_Air)). It has a long relationship with **LATAM** and has used **Iberojet** wet leases going the other way ([Aviacionline](https://aviacionline.com/iberojet-wamos)).
- **Game story:** "Scheduled airline turned pure ACMI." That pivot is a strategic choice the player could make too.

### 1.5 Privilege Style (Spain)
- Palma de Mallorca based, 20+ years old, a small widebody and narrowbody ACMI and charter operator. Sources list a 777‑200ER, an A330‑200 and 2 A321s, plus a **767‑300ER EC‑LZO** ([Airways](https://www.airwaysmag.com/legacy-posts/privilege-style-20-years)). Passengers never book with it directly; it always flies for a contracting party.
- It was the UK Home Office's **"deportation airline of last resort"** (e.g. Jamaica) ([Corporate Watch](https://corporatewatch.org/?p=8691)). It was booked for the **first UK–Rwanda asylum flight on 14 Jun 2022**: 767 **EC‑LZO** at the MoD airfield **Boscombe Down**. The flight was **grounded minutes before departure** by a European Court of Human Rights interim measure, after one-by-one legal appeals had emptied the passenger list ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/06/15/1105161913/u-k-cancels-first-flight-to-deport-asylum-seekers-to-rwanda); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/06/14/uk-rwanda-asylum-seekers-flight)). The airline then **withdrew after outside pressure** ([Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/world/article-airline-quits-british-plan-to-deport-asylum-seekers-to-rwanda-amid)).
- **Game story:** "Controversial contract" mechanics: lawyers, protests, last-minute legal cancellation with crew already on duty, and a public reputation hit.

### 1.6 Titan Airways (UK)
- Founded on **20 Jan 1988** (Artac group). A **2012 management buyout** left founder Gene Willson as owner. HQ is at **Stansted**; ICAO **AWC**, callsign **"ZAP"**. It moved from Boeing to all-Airbus and retired its last 737 and 757 in early 2023. It flies A320/A321/A321neo, A321P2F and A330 freighters ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Airways)).
- **ACMI was about 62% of the business** at one point. It specialises in **short-notice sub-charter**, with an aircraft **airborne within 60 minutes**. It has flown for **BA at Gatwick** and **easyJet** (Gatwick/Berlin/Munich), and seasonal Gatwick–**Banjul**. It also flies for governments and the sports and entertainment sectors.
- **State aircraft:** It operates the UK government's VIP **A321neo G‑GBNI** ("Global Britain" livery) for the **Royal Family, Prime Minister and ministers**. Royal trips use royal callsigns ([ch-aviation](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/101940-uks-titan-airways-to-operate-a321neo-for-the-government); [UKDJ](https://ukdefencejournal.org.uk/private-firm-take-on-some-vip-transport-duties-from-raf/)).
- **Game story:** "The rescue airline." It earns its money from standby readiness, and its top prestige reward is the state contract.

### 1.7 Air Atlanta Icelandic (Iceland + Air Atlanta Europe, Malta)
- Founded on **10 Feb 1986** by **Capt. Arngrímur Jóhannsson and Þóra Guðmundsdóttir** with one wet-leased **707**. Its first contract was **Caribbean Airways, London–Barbados**. Hajj began in **1988 with Air Afrique**. **Saudia 747s since 1993**, and it now calls itself **"one of the largest ACMI Hajj operators in the world"**. It went all-widebody by 1999. Clients over the years include Sudan Airways, Lufthansa, Finnair, Britannia, Iberia, BA, Air India, Cargolux and National ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Atlanta_Icelandic)). It flew **UN peacekeeper airlifts in 1992** and a **1996 sky wedding**.
- **Band tour: "Ed Force One"** for **Iron Maiden's Book of Souls tour, 2016**. The aircraft was 747‑400 **TF‑AAK**, painted in tour livery in Feb 2016 and flown by the singer **Bruce Dickinson, a qualified airline pilot**. The tour made **50 stops in 35 countries for 47 concerts**. The band had outgrown its earlier **757s from Astraeus**. On **12 Mar 2016 at Santiago**, a towing steering pin failed and the aircraft **hit a tug, damaging two engines and the gear**. It was **back in service on 22 Mar** (10 days of AOG mid-tour) ([Australian Aviation](https://australianaviation.com.au/2016/05/iron-maidens-ed-force-one-touches-down-in-brisbane/); [ACS blog](https://www.aircharterservice.com/about-us/news-features/blog/backstage-on-ed-force-one)).
- **Recent:** Air Atlanta Europe (Malta) wet-leased its whole 9H fleet to Saudia. The passenger 747s **retired after the 2022 Hajj**. The first **777‑200ER came in 2025**, and **Atlas Air agreed to buy 49% in May 2026**.
- **Game story:** "Hajj kings with jumbo jets." This profile drives the Hajj season mechanic and the rock-tour contract.

### 1.8 Omni Air International (USA)
- Tulsa based, FAR Part 121, part of ATSG. It started in **1993** as Omni Air Express (727 freighters), moved to passenger DC‑10s in 1997, 757s in 2003, and in **2011 the 777‑200ER**. It was the **first US non-scheduled carrier approved by the DoD's Commercial Airlift Review Board for 777 passenger flights**. By then it had moved **over 5 million military troops** (first passenger approval in Feb 1999) ([PRNewswire](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/omni-air-international-inc-receives-dod-carb-approval-to-operate-b777-200er-145338495.html)). Fleet in 2021: **15 767s and 777s**.
- **Kabul 2021:** Activated under the **Civil Reserve Air Fleet (CRAF) Stage I** with **three 777‑200s** ([ATSG](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210823005385/en/ATSG-to-Support-the-United-States-in-Afghanistan)). It also flies ICE flights.
- **Game story:** "The troop mover." The player gets steady government block hours, but must hold a defence approval (an audit gate) and accept being called up in a crisis.

### 1.9 National Airlines (USA)
- A cargo and passenger military charter operator. Its **747‑400BCF crashed just after takeoff from Bagram on 29 Apr 2013**, killing all **7 crew**. The cause was **inadequately restrained MRAP armoured vehicles** (5 heavy vehicles) shifting aft. The NTSB also cited **weak FAA oversight** ([AVweb](https://avweb.com/flight-safety/ntsb-loose-cargo-caused-fatal-747-crash/)).
- **Game story:** This is the sober side of military cargo. Loadmaster skill and load-securing audits matter, and a cheap shortcut can be catastrophic. Keep it as a low-probability, high-impact risk tied to rushed or under-trained loading; do not trivialise it.

### 1.10 Global Crossing Airlines, "GlobalX" (USA)
- Founded in **Sept 2018** by Ed Wegel, with operations from **Aug 2021**, based in Miami. Fleet: **10 A320, 4 A321, 4 A321P2F** (Aug 2025). 2025 revenue was **US$246 m** with a **net loss of $3.05 m** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Crossing_Airlines)). It does sports charters (NCAA teams, MLS) and Cuba flights (CubaX).
- **ICE deportation:** It flew **over half of all ICE removal flights in 2024–25**. ICE was **more than half of its revenue in Q2 2025** ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/chart/35096/monthly-ice-flights-by-flight-purpose)).
- **Controversies:**
  - Jan 2025 Brazil flight: repeated tech faults, an unscheduled landing, passengers fainting in the heat, and a diplomatic row.
  - Mar 2025 flights via Honduras to El Salvador, flown despite a court order.
  - Flight attendants worried about **evacuating shackled passengers**.
  - A May 2025 hack that defaced its website.
- **Game story:** This is the concentration-risk lesson. One client, one controversial mission type, and enormous exposure if politics turns.

## 3. Day-to-day problems and surprises (random event fuel)

### 3.1 Crew out of hours: the most common real ACMI headache
EASA rules ([ORO.FTL](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/easy-access-rules-air-operations)), simplified for game rules:
- **Max basic flight duty period (FDP):** **13 h** for acclimatised crew starting 06:00–13:29 with 1–2 sectors. It drops by **30 min per extra sector** (min 9 h) and is shorter for early or late starts.
- **Planned extension:** +1 h, at most twice in 7 days. **Commander's discretion** on the day: **up to +2 h** (+3 h with augmented crew). The commander must file a report, and overuse should flag the operator.
- **Augmented crew** (3–4 pilots plus a bunk): max FDP **16–17 h**. That is what makes ultra-long-haul possible and why "heavy crews" cost more.
- **Cumulative limits:** **60 duty h in 7 days, 110 in 14, 190 in 28**. **100 flight h in 28 days, 900 per calendar year, 1,000 in 12 months**. This caps how many block hours each crew can deliver a year and drives the **crew-sets-per-aircraft ratio** (about 5–6 crew sets per narrowbody, 8–12 per widebody (est.)).
- **Rest:** At home base, at least as long as the preceding duty or **12 h**. Away, as long as the duty or **10 h**, including an 8 h sleep opportunity. **A 36 h weekly rest with 2 local nights** at least every 168 h.
- **Typical cascade:** a 2 h ATC slot delay → the return leg would end beyond the FDP → crew "go illegal" → the aircraft and passengers night-stop, or you fly in a positioning crew → hotel for 180 passengers → a compensation claim lands on the client and the client invoices you.

## 4. The flight attendant's view of ACMI life

What the work is like, assembled from operator statements, crew-reported themes and the user's own background. Numbers marked (est.) are typical ranges from crew forums and recruitment ads, not audited figures.

### 4.1 The shape of life
- **Seasonal deployment:** A crew is sent to a client base (e.g. Hamburg for Eurowings, Toronto for Swoop, Cancún or Punta Cana flows, Jakarta or Medina for Hajj) for **weeks to a whole season**. ACMI narrowbody contracts often run **April/May–October**. Winter is quieter: rest, training, part-time work or winter deployments (Canada, Caribbean, southern hemisphere). GetJet says crews rest with their families after the summer peak.
- **Typical rhythm on a wet-lease base (est.):** 5 on / 2–3 off, or 6 on / 3 off. Days alternate between two "turnaround" sectors (e.g. HAM–PMI–HAM) and longer 4-sector days. The crew lives in a contracted hotel for the whole deployment, with **rotations home every 3–6 weeks**.
- **Widebody ACMI / charter (EuroAtlantic style):** Long and irregular trips. A long-haul charter may be a **3–10 day pairing** with long layovers in places tourists never see (Dili, Bissau, Kano, Port Harcourt, Medina, Manaus). Hajj means a **month-plus based abroad**. Then a VIP flight next week, a military rotation after that, and a scheduled-service wet lease for an airline.
- **Per diems / allowances (est.):** About **€40–€90 per day away**, varying by country cost and company. Some operators pay the per diem per layover night, others per day on deployment. Hotel breakfast may or may not be included. Late or reduced per diems are a classic grievance.
- **Pay (est.):** Eastern European ACMI cabin crew have a low base (about **€700–€1,500/month**) plus sector or block-hour pay and per diems. Southern European widebody charter pay is higher per month but irregular. Seasonal contracts are common and **winter unpaid leave** is a known pain point.
- **Mixed crews:** GetJet crews come from **15 nationalities**. The common language is English, but **PAs must be in the client's language**, so a "language speaker" (often supplied by the client) may fly on board. Passengers complain when the crew doesn't speak German, Spanish, Portuguese or Arabic.
- **Uniforms and brands:** Depending on the contract, crew wear **their own uniform on a client-branded aircraft**, or the **client's uniform**. Sometimes the aircraft is in the operator's livery with client stickers by the door. Crew learn each client's **service flow**: buy-on-board vs complimentary, trolley order, specific announcements, brand rules on scarves or hair. Switching clients every few weeks is mentally tiring but fun to show.
- **Hotels:** These are the biggest morale lever. City-centre hotels with a pool are good; an airport box with no food after 21:00 and a 40-minute shuttle is bad. On long deployments, crew care about **laundry, a kitchen, gym, safe neighbourhood and Wi‑Fi**.

### 4.2 Service standards by mission type (FA view, gameable)
| Mission | Service style | What crews notice |
|---|---|---|
| **VIP / presidential** | Bespoke catering, often the client's own chef or caterer; china and linen; protocol order of service; the delegation leader decides timings; no fixed menu. | Pressure, last-minute changes, security staff with weapons on board, press rows, very long duty days. **Prestige and sometimes gifts.** The senior cabin crew member is hand-picked. |
| **Military / troop rotation** | Simple hot meals, lots of water and snacks; troops often carry personal weapons (bolts removed, stowed per rules); multi-stop routes (e.g. via Shannon, Leipzig, Kuwait). | Polite and grateful passengers, but austere bases, long ground times, war-risk areas. Crews often say they love these flights. |
| **Hajj / Umrah** | Halal catering, **no alcohol**, segregated seating requests, **prayer times**, **the Miqat announcement** so pilgrims can enter the state of Ihram before crossing the boundary, Zamzam water carriage rules, heavy cabin baggage. | Many elderly first-time flyers who need help with lavatories, seat belts and oxygen; heat at Jeddah; long boarding. Emotionally powerful flights. Female crew dress codes may be set by the client. |
| **Deportation / return** | Minimal service; escorts manage the returnees; restraints; no metal cutlery; doors and lavatory supervision. | Moral distress, risk of violence, emergency-evacuation concerns with shackled passengers (GlobalX FA reports), activists at the airport. Some crew refuse. Morale cost unless the crew opt in. |
| **Refugees / evacuation / repatriation** | Families, babies, pets, people with almost no luggage; medical cases. | Very meaningful. Crews remember these for life. Chaotic boarding and documents; an exhausting but high-morale event. |

### 4.3 What makes ACMI crew happy or unhappy (→ morale model)
- **Happy:** Good hotels and decent per diems paid on time. Rosters published early and stable. Fair distribution of "nice" trips (exotic layovers, VIP, sports, Antarctica). Flying with the same crew family for a season. Time at home between deployments. Recognition from the client. Meaningful missions (repatriation, refugees, humanitarian). Seniority paths (FA → senior FA → purser/CSD → trainer).
- **Unhappy:** Out-of-hours delays and last-minute roster changes. Shared rooms or poor hotels. Unpaid winters. Delayed per diems. Being treated as invisible by the client. Controversial missions (deportations) without opt-out. Visa hassles. Fatigue from long duty days at commander's discretion. Being moved between clients and uniforms every few days. Unpaid standby.

### 4.4 What a former ACMI FA would love to see in the game
1. **Named crew with personalities and languages.** Portuguese, Spanish, Lithuanian, Arabic, French, Russian or Hindi speakers unlock client fit and morale bonuses.
2. **A "crew bag" view of a rotation:** days away, cities visited, hotel rating, per diem earned, and the next trip home.
3. **Uniform swapping:** the same FA shown in the company uniform, then a client's uniform (e.g. a Hajj client's modest uniform, or a VIP suit).
4. **Layover city moments:** "Crew dinner in Bissau", "pool day in Fortaleza", "snow in Toronto", "Medina at dawn". Small random flavour texts that raise morale.
5. **The senior cabin crew member (CSD/purser) as a key role.** A strong CSD fixes VIP and Hajj complications; a weak one escalates them.
6. **Real-sounding announcements:** the Miqat PA on Hajj flights, the "ladies and gentlemen, this flight is operated by…" line on wet leases, the language-speaker PA.
7. **On-board incidents:** a medical emergency (a doctor on board?), unruly passenger, birth on board, a VIP whose suitcase didn't make it, Zamzam water leaking from the hold.
8. **Seasons as chapters:** summer wet-lease base, Hajj, winter Caribbean, the polar charter.
9. **Fatigue made visible:** a crew member near their duty limits flagged before the player accepts a delay.
10. **Pride missions:** COVID repatriation and refugee flights generating "stories" that raise company morale and reputation.

### 5.4 Industry jargon (glossary for UI text and tooltips)
| Term | Meaning (game-ready) |
|---|---|
| **ACMI** | Aircraft, Crew, Maintenance, Insurance: the lessor provides all four, the client pays per **block hour** and covers fuel, landing fees, handling, catering and navigation charges (usually). |
| **Wet lease** | Roughly ACMI (sometimes with cabin crew from the lessee: "**damp lease**"). |
| **Dry lease** | Aircraft only; the lessee operates it on its own certificate. |
| **Full charter / ad hoc charter** | The operator sells the whole aircraft for a price per flight **including fuel and fees**; the client takes the seats. |
| **Sub-charter / sub-service** | The operator hires another carrier to fly a contracted flight (AOG, crew, capacity). |
| **Block hour (BH)** | Time from off-blocks (pushback) to on-blocks; the unit of ACMI billing. |
| **MGH** | Minimum Guaranteed Hours: the client pays for at least N BH per month even if it flies less. |
| **Positioning / ferry** | A non-revenue flight to bring the aircraft to the start of a job (usually billed to the client in ad hoc work, often not in ACMI). |
| **Deadhead / DHC** | Crew travelling as passengers to or from a duty; counts as duty. |
| **AOG** | Aircraft On Ground: grounded by a technical problem. |
| **MEL** | Minimum Equipment List: what may be broken and still fly (deferred defects). |
| **PIC / Commander, F/O, SCCM / CSD / Purser** | Pilot in command; first officer; senior cabin crew member (EASA term) or cabin services director. |
| **FDP / FTL** | Flight duty period / flight time limitations. |
| **Commander's discretion** | The PIC may extend the FDP up to 2 h on the day for unforeseen circumstances. |
| **Augmented / heavy crew** | Extra pilots with on-board rest for long FDPs. |
| **Rotation / pairing / trip** | A sequence of duties starting and ending at a base. |
| **Roster** | Monthly plan of each crew member's duties; **standby** (home or airport) and **reserve** days. |
| **Layover / nightstop** | Rest away from base; a nightstop also means the aircraft stays overnight at an outstation. |
| **Turnaround (turn)** | Flying out and straight back without a crew rest. |
| **Slot (ATFM / CTOT)** | Air traffic flow slot (take-off time); also **airport slot** (right to use runway or terminal at a coordinated airport). |
| **Permit / dip clearance** | Landing or overflight permission; diplomatic clearance for state flights; **PPR** = prior permission required. |
| **Handling agent** | Ground services at an outstation (stairs, baggage, fuel coordination). |
| **GD / crew list / APIS** | General Declaration, crew manifest, passenger data sent to authorities. |
| **Tankering** | Carrying extra fuel to avoid expensive or unavailable fuel at the destination. |
| **PNR** | Point of No Return (polar and remote flights). Also Passenger Name Record. |
| **ETOPS** | Twin-engine extended-range approval: needed for the 767/777/A330 over oceans and polar areas. |
| **C-check** | Heavy maintenance (weeks in a hangar, usually in winter). |
| **AOC** | Air Operator Certificate; **TCO** = EASA third-country operator authorisation for non-EU carriers. |
| **IOSA** | IATA safety audit many clients require. |
| **CRAF** | US Civil Reserve Air Fleet: airlines pledge aircraft for military call-up in exchange for peacetime DoD business. |
| **Preighter** | A passenger aircraft carrying cargo in the cabin. |
| **Ihram / Miqat / Zamzam** | Hajj: pilgrim state and garment; the boundary announced on board; holy water pilgrims bring home. |
